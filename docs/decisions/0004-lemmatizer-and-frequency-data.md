# 0004 — Lemmatizer and frequency data

Status: accepted (LING-001)

Lemmatizer. simplemma 2.0.0 (MIT, https://github.com/adbar/simplemma, PyPI) through `packages/pipeline/scripts/lemma_bridge.py`, spawned per batch by `src/lemmatize.ts` with JSON on stdin/stdout. Chosen over vendoring its tables (mixed ODbL / CC-BY-SA data, custom binary format) and over npm (no German lemmatizer exists). Pinned version; `LINGO_PYTHON` selects the interpreter (defaults to `python3`). Casing rule for sentence-initial tokens mirrors simplemma's own gated initial-lowering (see lemma_bridge.py). Tests use a committed lookup table generated by the bridge; the bridge smoke test skips when simplemma is absent; CI installs it in a venv.

Frequency lists. `data/freq-de.txt` and `data/freq-en.txt` are derived from hermitdave/FrequencyWords, OpenSubtitles 2018 `*_50k.txt` (CC BY-SA 4.0 for content, https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), lemmatized with simplemma, counts merged by lemma, tokens with digits/punctuation dropped, names (below) stripped, top 20 000 lemmas, one per line, rank = line number. The source lists are lowercase, and simplemma reads a lowercase German noun as a verb (haus → hausen, hat → haben) or not at all (stunden), so every German form is also looked up capitalised: the count is credited to both lemmas when they differ (Haus and hausen, Hat and haben — preferring the noun would send hat → Hat), except that the lowercase lemma must be attested by ≥ 1 % of the form's count in the list's other forms (bitten, danken pass; hausen, tagen, zimmern do not). Known cost: a real verb that shares a form with a common noun is over-counted (beten by Bett, landen by Land). Generated by `pnpm --filter @lingo/pipeline build:freq`; the derived lists are committed (~200 KB each) with a sidecar `freq-{lang}.meta.json` (source URL, sha256, licence, simplemma version, date). Because the lists are a share-alike derivative, they stay under CC BY-SA 4.0 and are attributed in docs/aws.md → Sources and data/README.md.

Names. No NER. `data/names-de.txt` and `data/names-en.txt` (first names, surnames, places, brands, holidays; seeded mechanically from the two dictionaries — rule in data/README.md — then hand-maintained and grown by the spot check) are stripped from the frequency lists at build time, so a listed name never has a rank, AND suppressed by the heuristic: a token is treated as a name and never highlighted when it is in the list for its language, or: en — capitalised and not sentence-initial (or sentence-initial and unknown to the lemmatizer); de — capitalised with an unchanged lemma and no frequency rank (the fallback for names the list misses; it only works because listed names — and, being absent from the corpus, most rare ones — have no rank). Numbers and number words (`NUMERALS` in highlights.ts) are never highlighted. This is a heuristic; the two-person spot check (PLAN §5) is the backstop.
